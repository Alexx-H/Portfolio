# Artifact Reflection – 3D Boids Simulation

---

## 1. Briefly describe the artifact. What is it? When was it created?

This project was a Boids Flocking simulation I worked on about a year ago. It is based on a project by Dan Schiffman, and the Boids concept was originally coined by Craig Reynolds in 1987. I made my own additions to it showing stress on the boids represented by color, and had interactive elements to adjust the boids as the program ran.

---

## 2. Justify the inclusion of the artifact in your ePortfolio. Why did you select this item? What specific components of the artifact showcase your skills and abilities in software development? How was the artifact improved?

I selected this project because I think that the Boids concept is a very classic simulation concept and showcases an ability to properly apply software engineering and development skills. I made various improvements to this code including boundary and object avoidance. I also ensured that the buttons and sliders were available at the top of the canvas to demonstrate the advantage of the octree (by being able to turn it off) as well as being able to adjust the three main variables of the boids algorithm.

## 4. Reflect on the process of enhancing and modifying the artifact. What did you learn as you were creating it and improving it? What challenges did you face?

I learned a lot about simulation in a 3D space, and dealing with large numbers of objects interacting in the arena. I saw first hand (and demonstrate it through the ability to turn it off) just how much computation it takes to compare each object to all the other objects and how structures like octrees mitigate that time complexity through spatial partitioning. The biggest challenge I had was trying to get the boids facing the right direction. Transforming the boid to face the right way of its momentum was actually trickier then the octree oddly enough.
